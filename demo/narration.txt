I'm V Varadharajan, and this is Faultline. It does two jobs on one engine. Ask a question, and it answers from the literature. Or hand it your draft, and three independent reviewers attack it before a real referee does. Seven model lineages, in opposed roles.

Here is the second job, on a real paper. This is SEVA, a detector for corpus poisoning in retrieval-augmented generation. Eight months of work, going to IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing. Nobody has refereed it yet.

It reads ten thousand words, pulls out nine empirical claims, and identifies the field on its own. Then three reviewers attack it, each on a different model family, so they do not share blind spots. Six major objections.

The method reviewer went at the headline number. The paper reports zero percent poison evasion with a ninety-five percent Wilson bound, and asked for the base rate, because without it that bound does not mean what it looks like.

The sharpest one questions the title itself. SEVA claims to be LLM-free, but it depends on pretrained embedding models. A referee can call that external model dependence under another name.

Now the other job, in a completely different field. Does creatine improve cognitive performance in healthy adults?

It searches four databases at once, screens twenty-nine papers, reads the ones that survive, and answers. Creatine modestly helps, especially during sleep deprivation. Moderate confidence, qualified consensus.

But the answer alone is not the useful part. Underneath it are five conditions that change it, each tagged with the axis it moves on. Population. Dose, and the benefit is dose-dependent. Setting, because most of the effect appears under sleep deprivation. Duration. And measurement, because the studies used different tests.

Every finding names the paper it came from, and it shows you the denominator: thirty-five retrieved, twenty-nine screened, and how many survived. A chat model gives you a handful of papers and no idea what it missed.

The stack. LangGraph for orchestration. Seven model lineages across Ollama locally, plus Groq and OpenRouter free tiers. Retrieval spans OpenAlex, Crossref, Europe PMC and arXiv.

The paper review took a hundred and thirteen seconds. The question took forty-four. Over ninety percent ran locally on one consumer GPU. Total cost: zero dollars.
